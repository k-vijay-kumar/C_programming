# IEEE 754 Binary32 (Common `float` Format)

C does not require every implementation's `float` to use IEEE 754 binary32. On systems that do, a finite normalized value is represented as:

```text
(-1)^sign x 1.fraction x 2^(exponent_field - 127)
```

The 32 bits are divided into a 1-bit sign, 8-bit exponent field, and 23-bit fraction field. The exponent bias is `2^(8 - 1) - 1 = 127`.

- Exponent fields `1` through `254` encode normalized exponents `-126` through `127`.
- Exponent field `0` encodes zero or subnormal values; subnormals do not have the implicit leading `1`.
- Exponent field `255` encodes infinity or NaN.

Thus, `-127` through `128` is not the normalized exponent range. Check the target implementation before assuming this representation or its layout.
